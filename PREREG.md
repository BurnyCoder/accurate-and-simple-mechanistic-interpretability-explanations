# Pre-registration: length-penalized detection scoring of SAE feature explanations

Author: Libor "Burny" Burian. Written and committed **before any scoring results exist** (pilot results are not used for confirmatory analysis). Any later deviation is listed in the "Deviations" section at the bottom, with the reason and the date.

## 1. Question

Detection scoring ([Paulo et al. 2024, arXiv:2410.13928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13928)) rewards an explanation only for its accuracy. The authors note that "current scoring methods do not account for interpretation length". We add a length factor and ask two things:

1. Does the resulting score rank explanations differently from plain detection?
2. Does it create an incentive for explanations that are both accurate and short? That requires an interior optimum, not a race toward one-word labels.

## 2. Method under test

For an explanation `e` of SAE latent `f`:

- `BA` is the balanced accuracy of the detection scorer on a balanced set of activating and non-activating contexts. It is `0.5·(TPR + TNR)`, computed by delphi's `compute_classification_metrics`.
- `J = 2·BA − 1` is Youden's J. Chance gives 0 and perfect gives 1.
- `L` is the number of tokens of `e.strip()` under tiktoken `o200k_base`.
- `S = max(J, 0) · 2^(−L/H)`, where the half-life `H = 20` tokens. **This is the pre-registered default.**

Why H = 20: it was chosen before any results. A typical existing Neuronpedia explanation (about 10 tokens) keeps about 71% of its J, a 3-token label keeps 90%, and a 40-token explanation keeps 25%. Other values of H are reported only as a sensitivity sweep, H ∈ {5, 10, 20, 40, 80, ∞}, where H = ∞ means plain J.

## 3. Data

- Source: [Neuronpedia public exports](https://neuronpedia-datasets.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/index.html), `v1/gpt2-small/6-res-jb/`. This is Joseph Bloom's residual-stream SAE on GPT-2 small, layer 6 (`blocks.6.hook_resid_pre`), with 24,576 latents.
- What we use from the export:
  - the existing explanations (`oai_token-act-pair` method by gpt-3.5-turbo and by gpt-4o-mini, plus a few human ones);
  - the activation records (17-token contexts with per-token activations).
- **Eligibility.** A latent is eligible if it has:
  - at least one gpt-3.5-turbo and at least one gpt-4o-mini explanation;
  - at least 45 activating records (maxValue > 0);
  - at least 25 records with maxValue = 0.

  Latents 6048 and 9396 are excluded, because delphi's detection few-shot prompt uses them.
- **Samples:**
  - Main: N = 200 eligible latents, drawn at random with seed 0.
  - Pilot: 10 different eligible latents, drawn with seed 1. These are only for debugging and are never analysed.
  - Reliability subset: 50 latents of the main sample, drawn with seed 2.
- **Example split per latent:**
  - Train: the 20 activating records with the highest maxValue. Only the Exp 2 explainer sees these.
  - Test positives: 25 of the remaining activating records. The remaining records are split into 5 equal-count activation-quantile bins and 5 are drawn from each (seed 0).
  - Test negatives: 25 records with maxValue = 0 (a random 25 of them if more exist; seed 0).

## 4. Scorer

- delphi `DetectionScorer`, used unmodified from [EleutherAI/delphi](https://github.com/EleutherAI/delphi), with its default prompt and few-shot examples.
- `n_examples_shown = 5`, `temperature = 0`, delphi's default shuffle seed of 42.
- Scorer model: `meta-llama/llama-3.1-70b-instruct` via OpenRouter, through delphi's `OpenRouter` client. This is the scorer family used by Paulo et al.
- If a batch's response cannot be parsed, delphi marks its samples `None`. Those samples are dropped from the confusion matrix. The failure rate is reported. An explanation that loses more than 20% of its samples is flagged and reported, not silently dropped.

## 5. Conditions

**Experiment 1 (existing explanations)**, per latent:

- `gpt35`: the gpt-3.5-turbo explanation. If there are several, the first in export order.
- `gpt4omini`: the gpt-4o-mini explanation, chosen the same way.
- `human`: human explanations where present. These are descriptive only (n is small).
- `top_token` (baseline): `the token "X"`. X is the most frequent max-activating token among the 20 train records, whitespace-stripped. Ties go to the higher summed activation.
- `random` (control): the gpt4omini explanation of another latent in the sample, assigned by a random derangement (seed 0).

**Experiment 2 (incentive test)**, per latent:

- Six explanations generated by `meta-llama/llama-3.1-70b-instruct` (temperature 0) with delphi's `DefaultExplainer` prompt, shown the 20 train records.
- Budgets B ∈ {2, 5, 10, 20, 40} words. For each, one sentence is appended to the last user message: "Your final explanation (the [EXPLANATION]: line) must be approximately B words long." (Amended before any results; see Deviations.)
- One more, `unconstrained`, uses delphi's default prompt as is.
- The analysis uses the actual token length L, not the nominal budget.

## 6. Hypotheses, tests and falsification criteria

All CIs are 95% paired bootstrap intervals over latents (10,000 resamples, seed 0). Tests are paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Holm-corrected within each hypothesis.

- **H1 (validity of the base scorer).**
  - Prediction: the mean J of `random` has a CI that contains 0, or |mean| < 0.05. The mean J of `gpt35` and of `gpt4omini` is > 0 with CIs that exclude 0.
  - Falsified if random explanations score clearly above chance, or real ones do not.
- **H2 (accuracy rises with length, with diminishing returns).**
  - Prediction: mean J(B=40) > mean J(B=2) (Wilcoxon p < 0.05). The mean J gain per nominal word from B=2 to B=10 exceeds the gain per nominal word from B=10 to B=40 (the CI of the difference > 0).
  - Falsified if J does not rise, or the gain per word does not shrink.
- **H3 (primary: interior optimum).**
  - Prediction: at H = 20, at least one intermediate budget B ∈ {5, 10, 20} has a mean S above both endpoints, B = 2 and B = 40. That means both paired one-sided Wilcoxon tests reach p < 0.05 after Holm correction over the 6 tests (3 intermediate budgets × 2 endpoints).
  - Falsified if no intermediate budget beats both endpoints, i.e. S is monotone in B.
- **H4 (selection incentive).**
  - Candidates per latent: the 6 Exp 2 explanations plus `gpt35` and `gpt4omini`.
  - Cross-fitting: each latent's test samples are split into halves A and B, stratified by class (seed 0). We select on A and evaluate J on B, then the reverse, and average the two.
  - Rules compared: argmax J, argmax S (H = 20), and argmin L (shortest; a baseline for the rule). Ties go to the shorter explanation.
  - Prediction:
    - the median L of argmax-S is ≤ 0.8 × the median L of argmax-J;
    - the mean J loss, J(argmax-J) − J(argmax-S), is ≤ 0.05;
    - the mean J of argmax-S exceeds that of argmin-L, with a CI that excludes 0.
  - Falsified if any of the three fails.
- **Exploratory (no confirmatory claim):**
  - whether `gpt35` and `gpt4omini` swap rank under S compared with J;
  - the H sweep;
  - length-unit robustness: Spearman of S computed with tokens vs words vs characters;
  - human explanations;
  - the relation between J and L across all explanations.

## 7. Reliability checks

On the 50-latent reliability subset, all Exp 1 and Exp 2 conditions are re-scored twice:

1. with a different delphi shuffle seed (43), which changes the batches (test–retest);
2. with `meta-llama/llama-3.3-70b-instruct` as the scorer (scorer sensitivity).

We report the Spearman correlation of per-explanation J and S between runs, and whether the H3 direction holds under the alternative scorer.

## 8. Stopping rule

No latents are added after results are seen. Calls that failed because of API errors (not parsing errors) are retried, once the main run ends, until the API failure rate is below 1%.

## 9. Known limitations, stated in advance

- Neuronpedia explanations were generated from top activations. Holding out the top-20 reduces, but does not remove, test overlap with what those explainers saw.
- 17-token contexts are short.
- 25 + 25 test samples per explanation make J noisy at the level of a single explanation. Paulo et al. used 100 + 100.
- One SAE and one scorer family.
- The ≥45-activating filter biases the sample toward denser latents (about 81% of latents pass).
- Token length depends on the tokenizer.
- H is a value judgment, the exchange rate between accuracy and brevity. It is not something measured.

## Deviations

1. **2026-10-29, before any results (pilot included).** The Exp 2 budget instruction changed from "must be at most B words" to "must be approximately B words long". Reason: delphi's explainer system prompt already says "Keep your explanations short and concise". Under an upper bound alone, the 20- and 40-word conditions would probably collapse to the same short length and fail to produce the length range that H2 and H3 need. The analysis still uses the actual token length L.
2. **2026-10-29, after the debugging pilot and before any main-sample results.** The test negatives change from Neuronpedia's zero-activation rows to verified random negatives.
   - What the pilot showed: Neuronpedia's zero rows are overlapping 17-token sliding windows over a handful of documents, shared across latents. The 210 sampled latents' 5,250 zero rows contain only 2,010 unique texts, and each latent gets about 5 underlying documents.
   - Why that matters: such negatives barely test precision, which is what penalizes broad explanations, the central trade-off of this study.
   - New negatives, as in delphi's default and in Paulo et al.: random 17-token windows from Skylion007/openwebtext (each with 47 tokens of preceding context, one random window per document, a pool of 4,000). A window qualifies if the latent's activation under the exact same SAE (sae_lens `gpt2-small-res-jb`, `blocks.6.hook_resid_pre`, run on GPT-2 small) is exactly 0 at every displayed token. Each latent draws 25 of its qualifying windows (seed 0).
   - Tokens are displayed in Neuronpedia's format, so positives and negatives cannot be told apart by formatting.
   - Unchanged: the positives, the latent samples and every analysis. The ≥25-zero-row eligibility filter is kept so the drawn samples stay identical.
   - The pilot is re-run with the new negatives. Its first run is discarded.
